# A two-spectrum sextic criterion for Weinberg-model quartic stability

**Specialist review synopsis · W3-BFB-EXACT · 9 October 2026**  
**Status:** internally checked analytic research draft; not externally reviewed.

## Problem and claimed result

For three electroweak doublets define \(K_{ij}=\Phi_i^\dagger\Phi_j\),
\(r_i=K_{ii}\), and
\[
 f(K)=\frac12\sum_i a_i r_i^2+
 \sum_{i<j}\left[b_{ij}r_ir_j+c_{ij}|K_{ij}|^2+
                   \operatorname{Re}(d_{ij}K_{ij}^2)\right]. \tag{1}
\]
All \(a,b,c\) are real; all \(d\) are unrestricted complex numbers. Pair order
is \(12,13,23\). The normalized physical domain is
\[
 \mathcal D=\{K=K^\dagger\succeq0:\operatorname{tr}K=1,
                                  \operatorname{rank}K\le2\}. \tag{2}
\]
Rank one is neutral; rank two is charge breaking. The central geometric object
is the **rank-one projector onto a charged configuration's kernel**. It turns
charged stationarity into a second instance of the neutral phase problem.

**Claim.** The minimum of (1) on (2) is obtained from two explicit spectra, each
containing three singleton values, at most six pair values and at most six
full-support values generated by a sextic. A scalar purity test selects physical
charged candidates. An ordered positive infinitesimal completion makes the
criterion valid for every coupling, including singular and flat-boundary cases,
and recovers a minimizing field direction. No field-orientation optimization
remains. This is a root/sign criterion, not a short list of polynomial inequalities
or a claimed production-ready exact scanner. The sextic bound concerns phase
elimination; residue annihilators need not have degree at most six.

## The common spectrum: definition and explicit roots

For real \(\alpha,e,g\), complex \(d\), unit-circle coordinates \(q_1=1,q_2,q_3\),
and \(r_i\ge0\), \(\mathbf1^Tr=1\), define
\[
 B_{ii}=\alpha_i/2,\qquad
 B_{ij}=\tfrac12[e_{ij}+\operatorname{Re}(d_{ij}q_j\bar q_i)],\qquad
 Q=\frac{r^TBr}{(g^Tr)^2},\quad g^Tr\ne0. \tag{3}
\]
All signs of \(g^Tr\) are allowed. Each retained candidate carries its value
\(\nu=Q\), amplitudes \(r\), phases \(q\) and projector
\(P_{ij}=\bar w_iw_j\), where \(w_i^2=r_iq_i\). Square-root sign copies have
the same values and admissibility.

Singletons give \(r=e_i\), \(\nu=\alpha_i/(2g_i^2)\). For each pair \(i<j\)
and \(\sigma=\pm1\), put
\[
 q_j\bar q_i=\sigma\bar d_{ij}/|d_{ij}|,\quad
 A_i=\alpha_i/2,\quad A_j=\alpha_j/2,\quad h=(e_{ij}+\sigma|d_{ij}|)/2,
\]
\[
 n=(A_jg_i-hg_j,A_ig_j-hg_i),\quad
 W=A_jg_i^2-2hg_ig_j+A_ig_j^2,
\]
\[
 r_J=n/(n_i+n_j),\qquad \nu=(A_iA_j-h^2)/W. \tag{4}
\]
Keep nonzero denominators, strictly positive supported amplitudes and
\(g^Tr\ne0\). Do not divide by \(\nu\) or by \(\det B_J\).

For full support set
\[
 s=(\operatorname{Im}(d_{12}q_2),\operatorname{Im}(d_{13}q_3),
                \operatorname{Im}(d_{23}q_3\bar q_2)),\qquad
 j=(s_{23},-s_{13},s_{12}).
\]
When \(\operatorname{Im}(d_{12}d_{23}\bar d_{13})\ne0\), phase stationarity
implies nonzero conserved current
\(r_1r_2s_{12}=r_2r_3s_{23}=-r_1r_3s_{13}\), hence
\[
                         r=j/(\mathbf1^Tj). \tag{5}
\]
Define
\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}
 e_{13}d_{12}&-e_{12}d_{13}&\alpha_1d_{23}-d_{13}\bar d_{12}\\
 e_{23}d_{12}&d_{12}d_{23}-\alpha_2d_{13}&e_{12}d_{23}\\
 \alpha_3d_{12}-d_{13}\bar d_{23}&-e_{23}d_{13}&e_{13}d_{23}
 \end{pmatrix},\quad z=(q_2,q_3,q_3\bar q_2)^T.
\]
The key identity is
\[
                             2Bj=\operatorname{Im}(Cz). \tag{6}
\]
Thus radial stationarity is reduced to
\(\operatorname{Im}(hz)=\operatorname{Im}(kz)=0\), with
\(h=g_1C_{2\bullet}-g_2C_{1\bullet}\),
\(k=g_1C_{3\bullet}-g_3C_{1\bullet}\).

For a row \((A,B,C)\), form
\[
\begin{split}
 P_{A,B,C}(t,u)={}&[\operatorname{Im}A(1-t^2)+2\operatorname{Re}A\,t](1+u^2)\\
 &+[\operatorname{Im}B(1-u^2)+2\operatorname{Re}B\,u](1+t^2)\\
 &+\operatorname{Im}C[(1-t^2)(1-u^2)+4tu]\\
 &+2\operatorname{Re}C[u(1-t^2)-t(1-u^2)].
\end{split} \tag{7}
\]
Use \(F=P_h\), \(G=P_k\) and
\[
              S(t)=\operatorname{Res}_u(F,G)/(1+t^2),\qquad\deg S\le6. \tag{8}
\]
The fixed factor comes from the nonreal common points \((i,i),(-i,-i)\).
Recover every common real root of the specialized quadratics, using all four
projective half-angle charts, and set
\(q_2=(1-t^2+2it)/(1+t^2)\), similarly for \(q_3\). Retain only positive (5)
and nonzero \(g^Tr\). Coprime bidegree-(2,2) forms have eight intersections
counting multiplicity; the fixed nonreal pair leaves at most six physical roots.

## Primal and charged spectra

Let \(A\) be the real symmetric matrix with diagonal \(a_i\) and off-diagonal
\(b_{ij}\). The self-adjoint operator
\[
 (LX)_{ii}=(A\operatorname{diag}X)_i,\qquad
 (LX)_{ij}=c_{ij}X_{ij}+\bar d_{ij}\bar X_{ij}
\]
satisfies \(f(K)=\operatorname{tr}(KLK)/2\).
The **primal spectrum** uses \((\alpha,e,d,g)=(a,b+c,d,\mathbf1)\);
every retained projector is physical and contributes its value \(\nu\).

For the second spectrum set \(R=L^{-1}\), \(G=A^{-1}\),
\(\Delta_{ij}=c_{ij}^2-|d_{ij}|^2\), \(g=G\mathbf1\),
\(\beta=\mathbf1^TG\mathbf1\). Then
\[
 (RX)_{\rm diag}=G\operatorname{diag}X,\quad
 (RX)_{ij}=(c_{ij}X_{ij}-\bar d_{ij}\bar X_{ij})/\Delta_{ij}.
\]
The **dual spectrum** uses
\[
 \alpha_i'=2G_{ii},\quad e_{ij}'=2(G_{ij}+c_{ij}/\Delta_{ij}),\quad
 d_{ij}'=-2d_{ij}/\Delta_{ij},\quad g'=G\mathbf1. \tag{9}
\]
For every retained \(P\), define
\[
 a_P=\operatorname{tr}(PRI),\quad p_P=\operatorname{tr}(PRP),\quad
 D_P=\beta p_P-a_P^2,
\]
\[
 K_P=(p_PRI-a_PRP)/D_P,\qquad E_P=p_P/(2D_P). \tag{10}
\]
Keep \(D_P\ne0\). Weighted stationarity gives \(K_PP=0\) and trace one.
Consequently \(K_P\) is physical exactly when
\[
 \chi_P=\frac{p_P^2\operatorname{tr}[(RI)^2]-2p_Pa_P\operatorname{tr}(RI\,RP)
                  +a_P^2\operatorname{tr}[(RP)^2]}{D_P^2}\le1, \tag{11}
\]
Equivalently compute \(\chi_P=\operatorname{tr}(K_P^2)\) directly.
A Hermitian trace-one matrix with a kernel has eigenvalues \((0,x,1-x)\), proving
the test. Include \(E_P\) for all passing candidates, not just the minimum of
the auxiliary quotient \(p_P/a_P^2\).

The coverage proof starts from charged stationarity
\(LK=\lambda I+\mu P\), independently obtained from the doublet equation
\(\Phi LK=\lambda\Phi\). Trace and kernel expectations give
\(\beta\lambda+a_P\mu=1\), \(a_P\lambda+p_P\mu=0\), yielding (10) and
\(\lambda=2f\). Its kernel equation is precisely projective stationarity of
\(p_P/a_P^2\). Sparse kernel supports are included by (4) and the singletons;
the symmetry makes off-support derivatives vanish.

## All exceptional inputs and the exact stability test

For generic coefficients, the phase forms are coprime and no charged stationary
point has \(a_P=p_P=D_P=0\). The latter is a real possibility, not excluded by
invertibility or a nonreal phase alone. Its generic exclusion is proved by
restricting \(p_P\) to the pole locus \(a_P=0\). On support size \(s\), that
locus has dimension \(2s-3\), while its stationary-zero condition has
\(2s-2\) independent value/first-jet constraints. The weighted inverse parameters
are independent: their linear map from the fifteen structured inverse-operator
coordinates has nonzero determinant 8192. These facts yield a proper algebraic
exceptional set; an explicit rational coprime example proves the corresponding
phase exception is proper.

To cover every original coupling, in coordinate order \((a,b,c,\operatorname{Re}d_{12},
\operatorname{Im}d_{12},\ldots,\operatorname{Im}d_{23})\), let \(M_j\) be its
coefficient monomial and use independent ordered infinitesimals
\(0<\epsilon_{15}\ll\cdots\ll\epsilon_1\ll\epsilon_0\ll1\):
\[
 f_\epsilon=f+\epsilon_0(\operatorname{tr}K)^2+
                         \sum_{j=1}^{15}\epsilon_jM_j(K). \tag{12}
\]
This is algebraic computation in an iterated real closed Puiseux field, not
sampling at small real numbers. The deformed coefficients avoid every proper
algebraic exception. If \(m_\epsilon\) is the smallest retained physical value
from the two spectra, then
\[
 |m_\epsilon-m-\epsilon_0|\le\tfrac92\epsilon_1,\qquad
 m=\min_{\mathcal D}f. \tag{13}
\]
The bound follows from \(|M_j|\le1/2\) for the three diagonal terms and
\(|M_j|\le1/4\) for the twelve others. Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\ f\ge0\iff m_\epsilon>0,\quad
 f>0\iff m_\epsilon>2\epsilon_0,\quad
 m=\operatorname{st}(m_\epsilon)\ }. \tag{14}
\]
The boundary has \(0<m_\epsilon<2\epsilon_0\); outside the cone
\(m_\epsilon<0\). All comparisons are exact ordered-field comparisons.

A minimizing matrix is bounded, so its successive residues, taken in the order
\(\epsilon_{15},\ldots,\epsilon_0\), preserve PSD, trace, rank and its minimum
value. Each selected bounded algebraic root has a finite residue procedure:
compute an annihilating polynomial, divide its common infinitesimal power,
specialize, order its distinct real roots, and use midpoint comparisons to
select the correct residue. Univariate Thom encodings implement roots/signs;
no original field quantifier remains. This handles coalescing roots and
removable poles. A Schur-complement Gram factor of the limiting matrix gives
an exact negative or zero doublet direction.

## Verification interface and limitations

The full proof is in `THEOREM.md`; exact counterexamples and alternative
representations are in `DERIVATIONS.md`. Executed checks include arbitrary-symbolic
(6) and (8), twelve field-gradient identities, exact singular/purity/infinity
negative controls, an independently derived matching sextic, and a complete
benchmark minimum of \(-7/60\). Twelve floating sextic minima agree with separate
field minimization records within \(7.22\times10^{-16}\); that agreement is not
a global certificate. No proof assistant, external reviewer or exact end-to-end
software implementation has certified this work.

The strongest critical-review targets are the inverse-spectrum equivalence,
full-support current elimination, pole-zero genericity argument, and the
ordered-field-to-original-field limiting bridge. Tree-level quartic positivity
does not settle arbitrary quadratic terms on a flat direction, the physical
vacuum, or radiative stability.
